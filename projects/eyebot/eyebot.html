Eyebot

Here are some of my contributions as an R&D Software Intern at Eyebot.

01 / Glasses Detection

To personalize downstream perception, the first step in the pipeline was determining whether a user was wearing glasses. I built a classifier that operates on cropped eye and brow regions from each frame, using facial landmarks to normalize for head pose and distance from the camera before classification.

Because glasses vary widely in frame shape, thickness, and lens reflectivity, most of the effort went into curating a training set that captured a range of lighting conditions and glare, which were the leading cause of false negatives.

02 / Kalman Filtering for Face Tracking

Raw facial landmark detections are noisy frame-to-frame, especially during fast head movement or brief occlusion. I implemented a Kalman filter to smooth the tracked face position and keypoints over time, using a constant-velocity motion model to predict each frame's pose and correcting with every new detection.

This reduced jitter in the tracked region and kept tracking stable through short detection dropouts, which was critical for maintaining a consistent glasses-detection crop from frame to frame.
